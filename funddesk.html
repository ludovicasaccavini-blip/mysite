For families

Learn the credit in five short lessons.

About five minutes. Then check if your child can get a scholarship.

    What this credit is

    A new federal law lets people redirect part of their tax bill to scholarship nonprofits called SGOs. The SGOs turn that money into scholarships for K to 12 students.

    It starts January 1, 2027. Your family does not pay anything and does not need to file anything with the IRS.

    Think of it like a neighbor choosing to send $1,700 of their taxes to help kids nearby, instead of to Washington.

    Who can get a scholarship

    • Your child is eligible to attend a public school, kindergarten through 12th grade.
    • Your household income is no more than 3 times your area's median income, adjusted for family size.
    • Your state has opted in to the program.
    Treasury estimates about 95% of American children live in households under the income limit.

    Meeting the rules does not guarantee a scholarship. Awards depend on how much the SGO raises.

    What a scholarship can pay for

    • Academic tutoring, including for kids who stay in their public school
    • Qualifying after school and extended day programs
    • Special education services and therapies
    • Books, supplies and fees
    Example: a 4th grader at her local public school gets weekly reading tutoring. The SGO pays the tutor directly.

    How you apply

    You apply to the SGO, not to your school or the IRS. Expect a short form with your child's grade, your household size and proof of income.

    If you already get SNAP, WIC, Medicaid or free school meals, a letter from that program is usually enough proof.

    Applications open in 2027, once your state names its SGOs. Leave your email below and we tell you the day they open near you.

    Common worries, answered

    "It's only for private schools."No. Public school kids can use it for tutoring and services.
    "Donors will know my child."No. Donors can't pick or see individual students.
    "It costs us something."No. Applying is free, and so is the scholarship.
    "We have to pay it back."No. It is a scholarship, not a loan.

    Your information is used only to check eligibility and pay providers. It is never sold or shared with donors.

    Questions

    Is this a deduction or a credit?

    A credit. A deduction lowers the income you're taxed on. A credit comes straight off the tax you owe, dollar for dollar.

    What if I owe less than my gift?

    The credit can't create a refund. Whatever you can't use carries forward for up to five years.

    Do I need to itemize?

    No. You claim it whether you itemize or take the standard deduction.

    My state hasn't opted in. Can I still give?

    Yes. You can give to an SGO in any participating state and still claim the credit.

    Is the rule final?

    Treasury published proposed rules in fall 2026. Details may change before launch. We update this page when they do.
